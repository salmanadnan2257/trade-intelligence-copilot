# HS codes: structure and how classification works

_Authored explanatory summary for demo purposes. Not legal advice._

## 1. What an HS code is
The Harmonized System (HS) is an international product-naming standard maintained by the World Customs Organization. Almost every trading country, including Pakistan, classifies goods for customs using HS codes. Correct classification decides the duty rate, any restrictions, and the statistics a shipment is recorded under.

## 2. Reading the digits
The first two digits are the chapter, the first four the heading, and the first six the subheading. The six-digit level is common worldwide. Countries then add more digits for their own tariff lines. In this dataset, sugar and sugar products sit in chapter 17: 1701 is cane or beet sugar, 1702 covers other sugars such as glucose, fructose, lactose and syrups, 1703 is molasses, and 1704 is sugar confectionery.

## 3. Why classification matters
A wrong HS code can mean the wrong duty, a missed import restriction, or a rejected declaration. When a product could fit more than one heading, classifiers use the General Rules of Interpretation and, where available, official rulings. For any specific product, confirm the tariff line against the current Pakistan Customs Tariff.
