# Customs duty concepts: ad valorem, specific, and tariff lines

_Authored explanatory summary for demo purposes. Not legal advice._

## 1. How duty is charged
Customs duty is charged either ad valorem (a percentage of the assessed value), specific (an amount per unit of quantity such as per kilogram or per metric tonne), or a mix. The rate attaches to the HS tariff line, so classification and valuation together decide the amount owed.

## 2. Customs value
The customs value is normally based on the transaction value, the price actually paid or payable, adjusted by valuation rules. Under-declaring value to reduce duty is an offense. Trade records that show value and quantity, such as USD value and kilograms, let an analyst compute an effective unit price.

## 3. Layers of charges
On top of basic customs duty, an import may attract additional charges such as regulatory duty, additional customs duty, sales tax, and withholding tax, depending on the item and current policy. Because these change, confirm the current stack of charges for the specific tariff line rather than assuming a single number.
